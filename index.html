Revenue · Step 1

Revenue changed. Was it Price, or Volume + Mix?

At this level, “Volume + Mix” intentionally combines total quantity and sales composition.

Revenue · Step 2

Now isolate pure Quantity from Total Mix.

Quantity asks: what if total units changed, but the Period‑1 composition stayed exactly the same?

Revenue · Step 3

Split Mix into where and what.

Channel Mix = where volume moved. Product Mix = what products gained or lost share inside each channel.

Gross Profit · Step 1

Gross Profit adds one new force: Cost.

Price can help, Cost can hurt, and Quantity plus Mix remain combined at this first level.

Gross Profit · Step 2

Separate pure Quantity from Gross Profit Mix.

Mix is valued at Period‑1 unit margin so later Price and Cost changes do not contaminate it.

Gross Profit · Full bridge

Price. Quantity. Channel Mix. Product Mix. Cost.

This is the source-of-truth Mixed Reality bridge. Steps 1–5 are simpler groupings of these same underlying movements.

Interpretation Lab

Do not confuse contribution with performance.

Use the bridge to describe what changed, then add business context before judging why it happened.

Positive mix, lower GP

Product Mix +Gross Profit −

Can both be true?

Yes. Product Mix can help while Cost, Price or Quantity effects more than offset it. Say: “Mix moved favorably, but total GP still declined.”

Negative mix, higher GP

Product Mix −Gross Profit +

Does higher GP prove the Mix was good?

No. Price, Quantity or Cost improvement may outweigh unfavorable Mix. Keep the driver and total result separate.

Which view answers whether the remaining portfolio improved?
